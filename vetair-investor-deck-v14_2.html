11 · Beyond relocation

Pet readiness.

During a regional crisis, pets were stranded at the Oman border — owners had the money, but not the clearances. A rabies titer takes weeks; you cannot create one in an emergency. The same AI engine that runs a relocation can maintain a pet's time-locked clearances year-round, so it can move fast when it has to.

What stays ready.Microchip, current rabies vaccination, valid titer, assembled documents — everything with a waiting period, kept live by an agent watching expiry. The highest-value item: an unexpired rabies titer.
The honest promise.A maintained pet needs only a health certificate (1–2 days) and a trip permit in an emergency — not the months a titer demands. That is the difference between crossing and being turned back.
Why it matters commercially.Recurring, low-commitment, emotionally driven — and it captures the customer before relocation. A relationship, not a one-off transaction. Incumbents are built around bookings; they cannot easily offer this. Concept stage — demand not yet validated.

12 · The hard part, solved

Truly non-human — up to authorization.

Governments expose no APIs for pet import. No competitor has an integration — none exists to build. The platform is fully agentic up to those submission points, which a browser-automation agent completes the way a person would, with one human authorization at each irreversible step.

Agent does the work.Navigates the portal (MOCCAE, US CDC, Japan AQS, Singapore AVS), fills every field from case data, validates against the rule, gets to "ready to submit." No human data entry.
Human authorizes the click.Risk-graded: low-risk forms submit on a tap; high-risk steps (Japan's 40-day window, a paid CDC reservation) require explicit confirmation. A liability gate by design, kept permanently — not a limitation.
Status.Specified and registered as a roadmap agent. Not built — gated on reliable browser tooling. The defensible claim today: prepare autonomously, submit human-authorized; full automation as tooling matures.
